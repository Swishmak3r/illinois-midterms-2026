# Judicial policy comparisons: research and publication method

Checked November 1, 2026. This memo and `policy-method.json` contain the method and reusable source catalog, not political classifications of judges. The six topic boundaries were selected before examining individual retention judges' cases: monetary-bail systems, specific firearm restrictions, voting rules, abortion access/funding, immigration authority/process, and federal executive authority.

The useful public unit is **a ruling on a particular question**. Show what the judge decided, the legal reason, the judge's actual role, and later appellate treatment. Where the same precise question appears in a documented policy source, describe the overlap as editorial analysis. Do not convert selected outcomes into an ideological identity, partisan percentage, or voting recommendation. Illinois judges must apply law regardless of personal approval and must not allow political interests to drive their decisions. [Illinois Code of Judicial Conduct, Rules 2.2 and 2.4](https://jib.illinois.gov/code.html)

Suggested public copy:

> Read what the judge decided, why, and what happened on appeal. When a ruling addresses the same specific issue as a documented party or Trump administration policy, we explain the overlap. That comparison does not establish the judge's personal politics.

Use **Policy overlap in this ruling** as the heading, and **Documented Trump administration policy** as the Trump column label. Each column may independently be `aligns`, `in-tension`, `mixed`, `not-comparable`, or `insufficient`. These are not opposites by default. `Not-comparable` means the known questions differ; `insufficient` means essential evidence is missing. `Mixed` requires identifiable opposing components, not uncertainty.

An affirmative comparison requires all of the following:

1. A primary decision, precise paragraph/page citations, case/date/court, and verified attribution to the judge. A trial ruling described on appeal must be distinguished from the appellate court's own decision.
2. A precise question and holding, including governing statute/precedent and limits. State whether the result followed a binding constraint. Decide whether the proposed comparison concerns the rule adopted or only its effect.
3. A direct dated baseline addressing that same proposition. Cite the party's own position, not its opponent's accusation. Republican and Trump baselines remain separate.
4. Later-treatment research and a visible status date. Preliminary relief is not a final merits ruling; a remand is not automatically a substantive win. A reversed or vacated ruling remains historical and must show its later treatment alongside it.
5. A second reader's check of attribution, comparability, and status. When these gates fail, retain the useful case explanation and show the relevant limitation.

Ordinary rulings on release or detention, sentencing, credibility, evidence, negligence, family disputes, or legal procedure generally do not answer the fixed policy questions. Neither a criminal defendant winning nor losing proves alignment. The same applies to a governor's powers versus the different question of federal presidential control. An expressly adjudicated procedural right can be relevant only when the baseline concerns that exact right. Public Rule 23 orders may document the judge's work, but the site's legal description must retain their actual nonprecedential status. [Current Rule 23, amended June 3, 2025](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/650f76b5-3cce-40b9-acc3-d93b1aced5f4/file)

Two consequential baseline limits:

- The DNC's 2024 platform does not expressly establish cash-bail abolition; its bail language concerns profiteering. Do not fill that gap with an assumed national Democratic stance. Its official PDF moved to [this working DNC URL](https://democrats.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2024-Democratic-Party-Platform.pdf). The upload directory is not a new platform date.
- Broad gun-rights language does not establish a position on every restriction. The [DOJ's June 13, 2025 Barnett amicus brief](https://www.justice.gov/crt/media/1404361/dl) supplies a more precise Trump-administration position on Illinois restrictions on common-use firearms and related items. It is advocacy, not a court holding, and does not establish a position on every firearm offense.

The catalog includes current official URLs for the 2024 national platforms, seven Trump baseline propositions drawn from six administration topics, an additional 2026 bail-policy statement, Illinois judicial ethics, Rule 23, and the Illinois Supreme Court's pretrial-system decision. These are evidence of dated policy positions. Their inclusion does not certify every executive order's current enforceability. A pre-2025 ruling can at most be compared retrospectively with a later policy; it cannot be described as a response to that administration.

Maintain a search log for every researched judge: names searched, topics, sources and queries, date range, decisions reviewed, inclusions/exclusions, date checked, and unresolved gaps. Do not describe the retention roster as a reviewed rulings inventory. Published opinions are a selective record, and an unsuccessful search does not establish that no relevant rulings exist.

`policy-method.json` contains the detailed statuses, source dates/URLs/pinpoints, topic-specific exclusions, baseline statements, required case fields, and a case-level `policyOverlap` field contract. No production files were changed by this research task.
