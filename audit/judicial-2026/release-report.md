## Republican, Democratic and Trump-policy comparisons

The site provides documented political history and precise case-level policy context. It does not turn a conviction, detention, release or reversal into a partisan score. A judge may be applying binding precedent or a statute rather than endorsing its policy.

Three firearm examples receive detailed comparison with dated primary policy sources. Vaughan's temporary gun-ban ruling had an effect resembling a later Trump DOJ position, but involved different constitutional grounds and was vacated. Knecht's felony-stalking ruling has a limited practical similarity to a later federal rights-restoration rule, with materially different legal reasoning and scope. Barberis's drug-and-firearm ruling addresses a different offense from the later *Hemani* decision and Trump DOJ brief. Those differences prevent a reliable blanket alignment label. The court decision, administration's argument and current-law limits are separate.

Quinn's directly sourced 2023 statement about courts during the Trump years appears as a dated public statement, separately from her rulings. No political inference is drawn from personal identity. [Comparison method](policy-method.md), [independent policy review](policy-review.md).

## Review and practical limits

Independent readers checked all 200 included judge–case records against primary opinion text, captions and operative passages. Corrections include appellate-only arguments, forfeited claims, trial-versus-jury findings, revised pinpoint references and later legislation. The source-review records are [Will/appellate](policy-review.json), [batches 1–2](source-review-12.json), [3–4](source-review-34.json), [5–6](source-review-56.json) and [7–8](source-review-78.json).

These are selected public decisions, not complete dockets, a formal citator service, an estimated reversal rate or voting recommendations. Later remands and appeals are expressly unknown where not verified. A court document marked “Final” is not proof that all review is exhausted. Professional ratings are their publishers' views. The guide remains selective for county offices, referendums and write-ins; official address-specific ballots control eligibility.

The generated judicial data is reproducibly checked against its source ledgers. Software checks test roster coverage, source contracts, neutral missing-data behavior and browser rendering; they do not substitute for primary-source review. [Release QA](../../QA_REPORT.md).
