# Cook retention judges: batch 2 verified ruling examples

Checked November 1, 2026. 15 judges; 25 selected primary-source ruling examples.

Actual official PDFs read for each selected ruling; captions and paragraphs verify circuit-judge identity, own action and reviewing outcome. Outcomes are selected examples, not an exhaustive career/docket audit or statistical reversal rate. No personal political orientation is inferred.

## Kristal R. Rivers

- **In re K.M. (2018 IL App (1st) 172349)**: Rivers imposed two years of juvenile probation, including a blanket prohibition on gang contact and requirements to remove gang-, gun-, or drug-related social-media material and disable tagging. On June 29, 2018, the appellate court vacated the blanket gang-contact condition as overbroad because it could prohibit innocent, unavoidable daily contacts. It upheld the social-media condition as reasonably related to rehabilitation and remanded for a revised probation order. Justice Gordon partly dissented. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/f1d8065d-3ba9-42db-8922-cc4d92a837dd/file). A selected juvenile sentencing ruling, not a finding about Rivers’s personal views on speech or gang policy. The majority’s and dissent’s rationales must not be attributed to her.

- **In re Maurice J. (2018 IL App (1st) 172123)**: Rivers denied a juvenile’s motion to suppress firearm evidence after crediting an officer’s account that the driver had partly avoided a speed bump. She later adjudicated the juvenile delinquent for aggravated unlawful use of a weapon. On June 20, 2018, the appellate court reversed outright. It held that the officer’s belief in a traffic violation was objectively unreasonable, that the stop and resulting firearm evidence were unlawful, and that the remaining evidence could not sustain the adjudication. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/b8ecbfb9-400d-4250-994a-63e6c3c626d9/file). The appellate ruling concerned the legality of this stop, not the validity of a gun-control statute. The source’s corrected official version is used.

Selected public rulings, not a complete career record. Published appellate review overrepresents disputed decisions; these examples cannot establish a judge-wide ideology or reversal rate.

## Steven G. Watkins

- **People v. White (2020 IL App (1st) 171814)**: Watkins denied suppression of pills found during a police stop and frisk, then convicted the defendant of possession and imposed two years of probation. On March 31, 2020, the appellate court reversed the suppression ruling, suppressed the evidence and vacated the conviction. Justice Hyman separately concurred. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/0457fe17-65bb-4cff-b984-f45744b3accd/file). The quoted police testimony and the appellate justices’ broader constitutional commentary are not Watkins’s own policy statements.

- **People v. Rainey (2025 IL App (1st) 231769)**: Watkins convicted the defendant of aggravated domestic battery and unlawful restraint after crediting the complainant’s earlier recorded identifications over her trial recantation. He imposed a five-year prison sentence. On September 24, 2025, the appellate court affirmed. It found sufficient evidence, deferred to the supported credibility assessment and held that the challenged database-search testimony showed the investigation’s thoroughness rather than proving the truth of the database entries. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/2599e59e-3f0f-4513-a699-be51fe3d0185/file). The complainant is identified only by initials in the official opinion. This summary omits unnecessary private details. The decision did not decide a general domestic-violence policy proposal.

## Abbey Fishman Romanek

- **In re Marriage of Patel (2022 IL App (1st) 211650)**: Romanek ordered real-estate sale proceeds held in escrow pending unresolved support and fee claims, and prohibited spending those funds. On December 16, 2022, the appellate court affirmed and remanded with directions. It rejected the prejudgment-attachment and due-process objections, found the injunction requirements satisfied, and urged prompt resolution of the pending request to reduce support. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/10acca4a-3e1e-412a-bff5-786bdfcd0ee1/file). This was interim preservation of assets, not a final determination that all funds belonged to one spouse. The appellate equitable-power explanation must not be presented as a written opinion authored by Romanek.

- **In re Marriage of May (2024 IL App (1st) 221485-U)**: Romanek barred a spouse from pursuing marital-asset dissipation claims at trial because the notices did not specify when the marriage began its irretrievable breakdown; she denied reconsideration. On December 23, 2024, the appellate court affirmed the dissolution judgment in a Rule 23 order, holding that the statutory procedural requirements had not been satisfied. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/258a80c2-fee5-4e19-9d2f-3f6422cdd000/file). The published official file identifies this as a Rule 23 order and not generally precedential. It is a procedural family-law ruling, not a finding about the merits of all alleged asset transfers.

## Judith C. Rice

- **Keefe v. Poniedzielska (2019 IL App (1st) 180657-U)**: Rice denied a motion to dismiss an existing plenary no-contact order. On May 24, 2019, the appellate court affirmed because the appellant supplied an incomplete record and inadequate briefing. It presumed the trial decision had a proper legal and factual basis; it did not independently adjudicate the underlying contested facts. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/07acd2dc-a301-4e0d-bb87-10a5382a2684/file). Rule 23 order. An affirmance based on an inadequate appellate record must not be described as a merits endorsement of all underlying allegations.

- **Bouvel v. Duggan (2022 IL App (1st) 221320-U)**: Rice granted an ex parte emergency stalking no-contact order after the petitioner testified. When the case returned to her, she requested briefing on a motion to substitute judges before deciding the motion to vacate. On December 6, 2022, the appellate court dismissed the appeal for lack of jurisdiction: the respondent had filed but had not presented the motion to vacate for decision before appealing. This was not an appellate approval or rejection of the order’s merits. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/427df15b-ae8d-494b-b6a5-e7828a81191f/file). Rule 23 order. Official index citation is 2022 IL App (1st) 221320-U, matching docket 1-22-1320; the PDF first-page citation has a typographical 211320-U. Another judge, Amendola, handled intervening proceedings and must not be confused with Rice.

## Patrick Kevin Coughlin

- **People v. Nooner (2026 IL App (1st) 241030)**: At resentencing, Coughlin imposed the statutory minimum aggregate sentence of 56 years for murder and attempted murder, including firearm enhancements. He rejected the claim that the adult defendant should be treated as developmentally juvenile on this record. On June 24, 2026, the appellate court affirmed, including rejection of the as-applied proportionate-penalties challenge. The earlier 66-year sentence had been reversed in 2021, but that sentence was imposed by a different judge, who died before resentencing. The 2026 opinion also notes parole eligibility after 20 years. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/848492ce-efcb-445c-9a1d-82ee8b446d0b/file). Do not attribute the original reversed 66-year sentence to Coughlin. A 56-year nominal term here was the adult statutory minimum and came with stated parole eligibility, so the number alone is not a reliable severity or ideology measure.

- **People v. Norfleet (2026 IL App (1st) 260828-U)**: Coughlin denied relief from continued pretrial detention after an alleged shooting at a stranger near the defendant’s home. On June 29, 2026, the appellate court affirmed the continued detention and denial of relief. It found the record supported dangerousness and the inadequacy of less restrictive conditions, and rejected the disclosure claim. No later case-specific merits disposition was verified in this review. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/08e6186e-7487-4e9a-9927-3cda6df951b8/file). Rule 23 order. Detention is a pretrial decision under the statutory clear-and-convincing standard; it is not a conviction or proof beyond reasonable doubt.

Selected public rulings, not a complete career record. Published appellate review overrepresents disputed decisions; these examples cannot establish a judge-wide ideology or reversal rate.

## Megan Elizabeth Goldish

- **People v. Rivera (2024 IL App (1st) 240520)**: Goldish declined to review whether continued detention was necessary while an appeal of the initial detention order was pending. The First District reversed and remanded. It held that the circuit court retained jurisdiction and had to review continued detention at each appearance under section 110-6.1(i-5) and Rule 604(h). The earlier initial-detention orders had been affirmed in a separate appeal; this decision did not order release. Later trial proceedings were not verified. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/8f0f8ed4-d541-4f60-82bc-4fbb8e0f5798/file). This is a procedural jurisdiction ruling under the pretrial-release statute, not a ruling on the constitutionality or desirability of abolishing money bail.

- **Trinidad C. v. Augustin L. (2017 IL App (1st) 171148)**: Goldish admitted a young child's out-of-court reports of sexual abuse and entered a one-year plenary protection order for the mother and her three children. The First District affirmed, finding no abuse of discretion in the evidentiary rulings. The order ran January 17, 2017 to January 17, 2018. Later proceedings were not verified. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/e06ea0d4-f5b1-4a13-883b-f8fa84a640ad/file). The protection order and evidence findings concern the specific record; they are not a general policy platform.

Selected published appellate records, not a complete career record or reversal rate. Trial-court actions and reviewing-court outcomes are separate.

## Anjana M.J. Hansen

- **People v. Drew (2026 IL App (1st) 251647)**: Hansen vacated Drew's murder conviction and granted a new trial after an evidentiary hearing on newly discovered evidence. After the State dismissed the charges, she declined to grant a certificate of innocence under section 5-5-4(c), then certified the legal question for interlocutory review. The First District answered the modified certified question in the affirmative and remanded: a certificate is mandatory when the specified statutory requirements are met, without an additional affirmative innocence finding. This was a certified-question decision, not review of the 1998 conviction. Further proceedings were not verified. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/f15625ce-c688-4a8f-a934-c213b1e2b769/file). The appellate index lists the opinion as Released. Its procedural posture and the judge's earlier grant of postconviction relief must accompany the certificate ruling.

Selected published appellate records, not a complete career record or reversal rate. Trial-court actions and reviewing-court outcomes are separate.

## Robert D. Kuzas

- **People v. Harvey (2024 IL 129357)**: Kuzas convicted Harvey of misdemeanor unlawful use of a weapon and imposed 30 days in jail, with time considered served. The First District affirmed over a dissent. The Illinois Supreme Court also affirmed, holding that the license-status admission did not require independent corroboration under the corpus-delicti rule. Separate concurrences disagreed with parts of that reasoning. Rehearing was denied November 25, 2024. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/c7cf105c-f225-4718-b1f2-f2719bbaf04c/file). The case addresses evidentiary sufficiency and statutory licensing elements, not a constitutional challenge to concealed-carry licensing. The Supreme Court's reasoning should not be attributed to Kuzas.

Selected published appellate records, not a complete career record or reversal rate. Trial-court actions and reviewing-court outcomes are separate.

## Maritza Martinez

- **In re Marriage of Kriley (2025 IL App (1st) 241923)**: Martinez issued a two-year protection order for a father and child, required a further mental-health evaluation before in-person parenting time, and allowed supervised video visits. She appointed the guardian ad litem as supervisor and required the parents to share his fees. The First District affirmed the protection order and most conditions, but vacated the supervised-visitation portion for reconsideration of who should supervise and how fees should be allocated. It did not require a different ultimate result. Later proceedings were not verified. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/71faba90-b522-4290-95df-28fb6e1ab11f/file). A fact-specific family-protection proceeding; not a general ruling on mental-health policy or parental rights.

## Bridget Anne Mitchell

- **Carr v. City of Chicago (2025 IL App (1st) 241639)**: Mitchell denied a motion for a new trial after a jury returned a verdict for Chicago in a wrongful-death case arising from a police pursuit. The defense verdict was the jury’s, not a factual finding made by Mitchell. The First District dismissed the appeal as untimely. It did not affirm or decide the merits of Mitchell’s trial rulings. ## James P. Pieczonka

- **Amaba v. RFJ Land Management, LLC (2023 IL App (1st) 220549)**: Pieczonka quashed service on a landlord LLC and vacated tenants’ default judgment in their lease-contract case. The First District reversed and reinstated the tenants’ $5,543.85 judgment. It held the attempt at the registered office plus the subsequent inquiry, mailing and Secretary of State service complied with the LLC statute. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/ad52f406-7520-4128-b8ce-72268884c328/file). The tenants’ underlying allegations were not tried in this appeal. The reversal concerned statutory service requirements, not an across-the-board tenant or business policy.

- **Village of Mount Prospect v. Kurtev (2017 IL App (1st) 170493)**: After a bench trial, Pieczonka found the driver guilty of speeding and disobeying a traffic signal, rejecting a request to require the village to produce engineering studies proving the signs’ compliance. The First District affirmed, finding the officer’s testimony sufficient and that the traffic-device presumption was unrebutted. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/9cc86154-e78f-44e3-aa0b-380bb13e896e/file). A fact-specific traffic decision does not support a conclusion about Republican, Democratic or Trump alignment.

## Patricia O'Brien Sheahan

- **Glorioso v. Sun-Times Media Holdings, LLC (2024 IL 130137)**: Sheahan allowed a former state official’s defamation case against the Sun-Times and a reporter to proceed, denying their anti-SLAPP motion. The reporting concerned allegations surrounding Trump Tower’s property-tax appeal; she did not decide whether the reporting was ultimately defamatory. The First District affirmed. In 2024, the Illinois Supreme Court affirmed and remanded, holding that these articles were reports about a third-party investigation rather than acts seeking government action under the then-applicable Citizen Participation Act. It resolved that threshold objectively and expressly overruled the appellate court’s suggestion it remained a fact question. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/0f9ce3b1-4415-4fb7-b102-17e0f5ec8d95/file). Trump’s property was background to the reporting. This pleading-stage anti-SLAPP ruling is not a finding of Trump loyalty, a ruling on his tax assessment, or final liability against the newspaper. The cited decision applies the law it reviewed in 2024. Later law: Public Act 104-0431, effective August 21, 2025, expressly broadens press protections and changes the motive/sole-basis tests for actions commenced on or after January 1, 2026. The 2024 reasoning should not be presented as the general rule for those new actions.

- **Clanton v. Oakbrook Healthcare Centre, Ltd. (2023 IL 129067)**: Sheahan denied a nursing facility’s request to compel arbitration of claims arising from a resident’s injuries and death. The appellate court affirmed on a different ground. The Illinois Supreme Court also affirmed and remanded in 2023 because the contract’s termination-on-death clause ended the arbitration agreement. The Supreme Court did not adopt Sheahan’s unconscionability rationale as its ground for decision. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/ad51c45d-3cb9-4d5f-bdbc-8d2a2745c386/file). This decided the contractual forum, not whether the facility was negligent. A result favoring a claimant in one contract case is insufficient for party or Trump alignment.

## Michael A. Forti

- **In re Marriage of Durdov (2021 IL App (1st) 191811)**: Forti reduced a parent’s child-support obligation after finding that increases in both parents’ earnings were a substantial change in circumstances. The First District reversed. Because the agreed terms anticipated both parents’ earnings changes, those changes did not justify the modification on this record. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/8e159858-1be7-435a-9fef-81416c1a4afc/file). This is interpretation of an individual settlement and statutory support standard; it does not establish a general stance on family policy. Later law: Public Act 102-0823, effective May 13, 2022, changed section 510(a)(1): contemplation or foreseeability is not a factor or defense unless the future event is expressly specified in the court order or incorporated agreement. This 2021 decision should not be used as a statement of the amended general standard.

- **In re Marriage of Klose (2023 IL App (1st) 192253)**: Forti treated the home and three investment accounts as marital property, divided the marital estate equally, and later clarified an offset for one spouse’s withdrawals. The First District affirmed the property classifications, equal division, and clarification of the judgment. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/cf76625d-dad9-47c3-8e7a-391c2203c305/file). An equitable division under the facts of a marriage is not evidence of the judge’s partisan identity or Trump-policy stance.

## Nichole C. Patton

- **Hwang v. Pathway LaGrange Property Owner, LLC (2024 IL App (1st) 240534)**: Patton refused to force an injured assisted-living resident’s claims into arbitration and declined to sever the challenged contract terms. The First District affirmed on substantive unconscionability grounds, finding the combined provisions strongly favored the facility. It did not need to adopt every alternative trial-court reason. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/d63ab1aa-60e2-4d94-b035-abbf428fa5ff/file). This decided enforceability of a particular arbitration agreement, not liability for the alleged injury or a broad political ideology.

- **Gercone v. Cook County Officers Electoral Board (2022 IL App (1st) 220724-U)**: Patton ordered Carmen Navarro Gercone restored to the 2022 Democratic primary ballot for Cook County sheriff, while rejecting her constitutional challenge to the training-qualification statute. The First District reversed Patton’s judgment and reinstated the electoral board’s exclusion. It held the candidate lacked the required certificate when filing and that the electoral board could not second-guess the training board’s certification function. [Official decision](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/resources/0b2c9980-ad7f-49f3-a891-872e6be23e92/file). Rule 23 order, not general binding precedent. This was a candidate-qualification dispute within a Democratic primary; it does not establish Patton’s preference for a party or general voter-access policy.
