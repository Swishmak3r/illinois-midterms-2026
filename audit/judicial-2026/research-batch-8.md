# Batch 8 judicial research — November 1, 2026

The combined ledger `research-batch-8.json` matches all 17 candidate IDs in the assigned batch and contains 21 selected ruling records. Each cites an actual official Illinois Courts PDF, identifies the judge's own trial role, and separates it from the appellate decision. The cases illustrate adjudication; they are not a complete or representative career sample, a retention recommendation, or evidence of personal ideology.

Notable attribution checks:

- Gerhardt's *Price* firearm convictions were reviewed in an appeal that also rejected Second Amendment arguments. Those arguments were first raised on appeal, so that constitutional holding belongs to the appellate panel, not Gerhardt.
- Booras decided a later motion seeking release in *Asma*. Michael Nerheim made the initial detention decision.
- Felice's *A.G.* parenting decision was affirmed. A later related appeal changed support retroactivity while also reversing separate contempt and name-change decisions made by successor judge Maureen Riordan. The latter are not attributed to Felice.
- O'Connell's *Stewart* reversal concerned proof of a qualifying prior felony, not the constitutionality of firearm restrictions.
- Parkhurst's *Cornelius W.* involuntary-medication order was reversed for missing statutory evidence. Her *Andrade* decision applies Illinois immunity for inadequate police protection, not federal qualified immunity.
- Campanella's *Everitt* reversal required a new trial before a different judge after a defective jury waiver. The appellate court did not decide the Second Amendment challenge.
- Hitzemann's *Jenkins* ruling reversed another judge's new-trial order; the appellate court reinstated the new-trial result based on the proper scope of posttrial review.

All 21 examples are marked not comparable to the defined party/Trump policy questions. None supports a judge-wide numerical alignment score. Individual detention, suppression, sentencing, statutory-proof, family-law, and procedural outcomes do not establish a political preference.

Later proceedings beyond the specified cited decisions were not comprehensively reconstructed; the case-level limitations make that explicit.
