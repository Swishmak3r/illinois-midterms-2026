# Independent source review: retention research batches 5 and 6

Checked November 1, 2026. **32 judges; all 47 included case records reviewed. No material holding, attribution or outcome errors remain.**

The reviewers compared the summaries with official court PDFs, including captions, judge roles, operative trial-history and legal-analysis paragraphs, and dispositions. The report does not certify complete trial dockets, all later appeals, or present-day validity of every proposition. Primary URLs and the reviewed pinpoints are recorded per case in `source-review-56.json`.

## Corrections and integration requirements

- **In re K.D.:** the batch6 owner added the distinction between forfeiture of the reasonable-efforts challenge and alternative merits review of reasonable progress.
- **Date labels:** preserve `dateMeaning` and distinct trial-action dates. Grischow’s Allen date and Noll’s Collazo date identify trial orders, not appellate opinion dates.
- **People v. Woods:** later Morgan (2025) changed appellate detention-review standards. The historical Reif summary makes no current-standard claim, but the caveat should match the Knecht profile. Morgan is not a later appeal in Woods.
- **Accuracy Firearms:** Vaughan’s 2023 decision granted interim equal-protection relief, was later vacated, and is not operative precedent. The 2025 dismissal and the uncertainty over the exact vacatur day are preserved.
- **Allen:** Grischow’s vaccination/testing TRO was vacated. It is not a final universal prohibition of vaccination requirements.
- **Collazo:** Noll’s relief was limited to the named plaintiffs for the 2024 election. The Supreme Court’s evenly divided dismissal after recusals is not a merits precedent.
- **Kloeppel:** Bohm’s local appointment ruling was reversed; the Supreme Court unanimously affirmed the reversal. Local county authority is not federal presidential power.
