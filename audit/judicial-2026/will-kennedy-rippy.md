Kennedy's selected decisions include both exclusion of prosecution evidence and refusals of defense requests. Rippy's examples include enforcement of search limits and a grant of police civil immunity. This is not a complete docket or a representative basis for a partisan score. The legal distinctions and appellate corrections belong beside each example.

**Lomeli requires dated legal context:** [Public Act 103-0032](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/publicacts/view/103-0032), effective January 1, 2024, moved the front-windshield object restriction to 625 ILCS 5/12-503(c-5) and prohibited stops or searches of a vehicle, driver or passenger solely for a violation or suspected violation of that subsection. The 2017 ruling concerns the earlier law; it does not establish current permission for a stop solely on that basis. The amendment is in Section 5, [official act PDF page 4](https://ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/PDF/103-0032.pdf).

In Pruitte, Justice Carter's dissent addressed both sufficient probable cause and, alternatively, the good-faith exception (paragraph 48); the ledger now includes both grounds.

The court's index labels Alkafaween as “Ween”; its PDF caption and final reporter page use Alkafaween. Its immunity issue concerns the Illinois Tort Immunity Act, not federal qualified immunity. Pruitte involved firearm charges, but its actual issue was search and seizure, not Second Amendment validity. Stellman is a nonprecedential Rule 23 order.
