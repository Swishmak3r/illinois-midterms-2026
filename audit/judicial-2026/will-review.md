# Will County judicial evidence review — November 1, 2026

The research ledgers contain 13 selected case records: two for Amy M. Bertani-Tomczak, three for Sarah Jones, five for Daniel L. Kennedy, and three for Daniel D. Rippy. Each identifies the circuit judge's action separately from the appellate or supreme court's decision. The sample is neither an exhaustive docket nor a reversal-rate study.

## Files and scope

- `will-bertani-jones.json` / `.md`: five rulings, original election evidence, source limitations and issue-specific search logs.
- `will-kennedy-rippy.json` / `.md`: eight rulings, including suppressions, defense procedure, police tort immunity, and involuntary commitment; source hashes and specific exclusions.
- `will-context.json` / `.md`: current 2026 ISBA/Will Bar advisory polls and original 2014 election-party evidence for Kennedy and Rippy.
- `will-index-*.json`: official opinion-index search metadata, complete pagination and page hashes. These are discovery records, not verified rulings. The Sarah/Third District search intentionally includes name variants and unrelated hits.
- `inventory-opinions-bertani-tomczak.json`: the inventory team's additional official-index discovery file; 137 name hits, not 137 attributed judicial rulings.

Raw downloaded court/election records and extraction files remain outside the repository in `../judicial-research`.

## Review controls

The pair researchers read the actual official court records. The coordinating researcher independently checked the trial action, role and disposition portions of Brusaw, Lomeli, Shepherd, Pruitte and Alkafaween. A separate policy reviewer checked the Bertani/Jones records and identified a precision correction in Green: the trial judge relied on the defendant's failure to object to an officer's recap, which must not be described as affirmative acknowledgment. The final ledger corrects that wording and records the appellate court's caveat.

The current ISBA page was independently read, including its methodology. Its Will County ratings are a joint advisory poll of lawyers, not institutional ISBA judicial evaluations and not political ideology ratings. Preserve the supplied percentages and respondent counts without recomputing one from the other.

The 2014 Clerk candidate roster was independently downloaded and extracted, confirming Kennedy's Democratic and Rippy's Republican ballot labels. Original 2008 and 1996 canvasses are documented in the Bertani/Jones ledger. Public profile statements must date these election facts. Do not turn them into claims about present affiliation, presidential endorsements, or beliefs. Directory codes should not supply party facts: their meaning can vary by publication, and the Will directory also contains a contradictory label for a current candidate.

## Material publication limits

Landerman's October 15, 2026 opinion is listed as a slip opinion. It orders further consideration of a constitutional sentencing claim; it neither exonerates the defendant nor invalidates the sentence. The official index also labels Sanders as Slip; preserve that observed status without assuming finality from its older filing date. Later appeal and remand outcomes are not fully certified for the other selected cases.

Crawford cannot safely be attributed to Rippy because the caption names two trial judges without allocating the appealed hearings. Travis's constitutional firearm challenge arose on appeal. A firearm charge in Pruitte is not a Second Amendment decision. Alkafaween applies Illinois statutory tort immunity, not federal qualified immunity. Brusaw's substitution motion was never ruled upon; it was not denied on the merits.

Lomeli's historical stop ruling must carry the later-law note in its ledger: Public Act 103-0032, effective January 1, 2024, added a prohibition against stops or searches based solely on the suspected mirror-object obstruction violation. The 2017 decision must not be presented as current authority to make such a stop. The independent review also corrected the Pruitte dissent description to include its alternative good-faith rationale.

The records support descriptions of particular legal actions and their consequences. They do not establish a categorical Republican, Democratic or Trump-aligned judicial ideology. Any comparison to a political policy must identify the exact shared issue, the distinct legal question, the sourced political position and the inference's limits. Unknown issue coverage must remain visible.
