# County and local candidate audit · October 28, 2026

This audit covers all 15 inherited county/local groups and all 31 former candidate/slate entries. The replacement dataset contains 48 separate county/local contests and 79 individual candidate profiles. The inherited entries contained 217 issue summaries and 113 endorsement labels, alongside unreferenced combined-slate finance figures. Every inherited group was examined. Combined slates were replaced with individual offices and people; unsupported issue, endorsement, biography, rating and funding claims were removed. Missing evidence means unknown, not a zero-dollar campaign, no endorsement, or a particular policy view.

## Corrections and scope

- County clerk, sheriff and treasurer candidates are now separate races. A party slate containing several people is not a candidate, and officials in different counties are not opponents.
- Race and candidate evidence links identify the election authority source. Brief retained policy summaries are attributed to campaign statements; they are not independent certification of accomplishments. Endorsements are retained only with a direct endorser record, with stated source limitations where applicable.
- All inherited local PAC relationships and fundraising/cash amounts were removed. An election roster does not substantiate finance totals, and the FEC does not regulate these Illinois county candidate committees. Independently verified state filings can be added separately with committee, period and source metadata.
- PVI figures, election ratings, assertions of battleground status, unsupported primary results and speculative county-board descriptions were removed.
- The fabricated statewide YES/NO referendum-coalition profiles were removed. No single statewide candidate or endorsement slate represents separate local public questions. The official sample ballot is the reference for a voter’s actual referendum and judicial-retention choices.
- This is a correction of the county offices already represented by the old groups, with several directly sourced countywide offices added. It is not a complete county-board, judicial, school-board or referendum directory, and it does not determine a person’s exact ballot.

## Southern and central counties

- Champaign: split the old multi-county slate into Clerk (Aaron Ammons / Alan Anderson), Sheriff (Dustin D. Heuerman / Gregory A. Worrell), Executive (Michelle Jett) and Treasurer (Byron Clark). Names and parties are from the clerk’s 2026 candidate table. This corrects the old narrative presenting Michelle Jett as already elected county executive. The full official table was available through the search index; direct retrieval returned HTTP 403, so that limitation is visible in the data.
- McLean: Clerk (Joshua Crockett / Kathy Michael), Sheriff (Jon Albee) and Treasurer (Rebecca C. McNeil), from Bloomington’s official November 3 specimen ballot. The countywide offices apply throughout McLean, while Bloomington and the county clerk administer different election jurisdictions.
- Madison: Clerk and Recorder (Rachelle Nicole Bauerle / Linda A. Andreas), Sheriff (Jeff C. Connor) and Treasurer (Mike Babcock), from the county’s linked 2026 general-election candidate PDF. The PDF was image-only and page 1 was rendered and visually read.
- St. Clair: Clerk (Laura Kaemmerer / Gary Hursey), Treasurer (Andrew Lopinot / Myki Rhodes), Sheriff (Richard “Rick” Watson). The clerk’s current filing tool lists Republican sheriff filer Steve Patterson as Objection Sustained; he is not represented as an active general-election nominee.
- Rock Island: Clerk (Maggie (McGrath) Crouch / Steven G Rugh), Sheriff (Marcus Herbert / Jamie M. Shoemaker) and Treasurer (Nick Camlin), from the official general-election sample ballot. The county website also links an older primary candidate PDF, which was not used to infer the general-election field.
- Jackson: Clerk (Frank L. Byrd / Doug Gill), Sheriff (Robert “Bob” Burns / Jay Wittenborn), Treasurer (Liz Stevenson) and Assessor (Tammy Ehlers), verified visually on page 2 of the official September 17 preview ballot for November 3.
